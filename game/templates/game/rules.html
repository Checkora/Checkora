Chapter 01

The basics

Chess is played on an 8×8 board between two players, White and Black. Each player starts with 16 pieces. White always moves first. The goal is to checkmate the opponent's King.

Starting position

Each side has

  • 1 King
  • 1 Queen
  • 2 Rooks
  • 2 Bishops
  • 2 Knights
  • 8 Pawns

White pieces start on rows 1–2, Black on rows 7–8.

Chapter 03

Piece values and scoring

Material values help explain trades and exchanges. A move that wins more value than it gives up is often favorable, though checkmate and king safety always outweigh raw points.

Reference score table
Rule of thumb: trade up when you can.
Higher score means the piece is usually more valuable.
Pawn
1 point
The basic unit of material. Often used to build space, structure, and promotion chances.
Knight
3 points
Strong in closed positions and can jump over pieces.
Bishop
3 points
Usually equal to a knight, but can become stronger on open diagonals.
Rook
5 points
More powerful in open files and often dominates the late game.
Queen
9 points
The most mobile attacking piece. Avoid trading it lightly.
King
Infinite
Ultimate value. If the King is checkmated, the game is over immediately.
Example: winning a rook for a bishop usually gains about 2 points of material, which is why that trade is often favorable.

Pawn special rules

Pawns move forward only but capture diagonally. On their first move they can advance 1 or 2 squares.

Chapter 05

Castling

Castling is the only move where two pieces move at once. The King moves 2 squares toward a Rook, and the Rook jumps to the other side of the King. Used to protect the King and activate the Rook.

Watch castling in action
Chapter 06

En passant

A special pawn capture. If an opponent's pawn advances 2 squares and lands beside your pawn, you can capture it as if it only moved 1 square. It must be done immediately: the chance is gone after the next move.

Chapter 07

Check and checkmate

Check: your King is under attack. You must resolve it immediately by moving the King, blocking, or capturing the attacker. Checkmate: the King is in check with no escape, and the game is over.

Chapter 08

Stalemate and draw

Stalemate happens when a player has no legal moves but is not in check. The game ends as a draw. Other draws: insufficient material, threefold repetition, or 50-move rule.

Stalemate position

Draw conditions

  • Stalemate: no legal moves, not in check
  • Threefold repetition
  • 50-move rule (no capture or pawn move)
  • Insufficient material
  • Mutual agreement

The Black King in the corner has no legal moves but is not in check. That is stalemate.

Chapter 09

Pawn promotion

When a pawn reaches the opposite end of the board (rank 8 for White, rank 1 for Black), it must be promoted to a Queen, Rook, Bishop, or Knight. Almost always promoted to a Queen.
